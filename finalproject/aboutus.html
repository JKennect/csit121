About the Studio

Founded somewhere between two bus stops in Baltimore, PixelForge Studios began when Alan Smithee realized waiting for the bus would be a lot less boring with video games. Armed with a laptop, questionable sleep habits, and a deep love for retro classics played through emulators that definitely existed for legal reasons, Alan set out to create the kinds of pixel adventures he grew up loving.

What started as “something to do while waiting for the bus” slowly evolved into a full indie studio dedicated to handcrafted worlds, nostalgic platformers, and games packed with enough charm to make your old CRT monitor proud. Today, PixelForge Studios continues forging retro-inspired adventures one pixel at a time preferably before the next bus arrives.

That passion for retro-inspired adventures eventually led to the studio’s breakout hit, Kingdom Quest. Packed with sprawling pixel-art kingdoms, dangerous monsters, hidden secrets, and an entirely unreasonable number of breakable barrels, the game quickly became a favorite among players looking for classic adventure vibes with modern indie charm. Its memorable world, lovable characters, and soundtrack capable of permanently occupying your brain earned Kingdom Quest the prestigious “S Tier” rating from TierDestroyers.Net — a title considered highly respectable by at least several extremely online gamers.

Following the success of Kingdom Quest, PixelForge Studios continued expanding its strange little universe with even more pixel-powered adventures. One of the studio’s most recognizable projects, Shark Survival Crafting, dropped players into the middle of a shark-infested ocean armed with nothing but a raft, crafting supplies, and rapidly declining confidence. The game’s iconic shark design became so popular that it eventually inspired the studio’s logo — proving that near-constant aquatic terror can still make for excellent branding.

The studio also introduced Brave Bark, a heartfelt platforming adventure following a courageous dog from Maryland who mysteriously finds himself lost in the same fantasy world as Kingdom Quest. Wearing his trademark blue neckerchief and fueled almost entirely by determination and barking, Brave Bark quickly became a fan-favorite character and helped expand the growing PixelForge universe. Players especially enjoyed discovering how the cheerful canine’s journey would eventually connect to the future hero of Kingdom Quest — along with the realization that apparently interdimensional travel can happen to anyone.

Location

The Studio's Location

Based in the heart of Baltimore, PixelForge Studios proudly operates from the city where it all began: a bus stop, a laptop, and a dream slightly larger than Alan Smithee’s budget. While other studios chased expensive office space in Washington or sleek tech buildings in Arlington, Alan decided to stay right where he was, mostly because rent prices suggested otherwise.

What started as a financial limitation eventually became part of the studio’s identity. Surrounded by Baltimore’s character, history, and occasional aggressively confusing traffic patterns, PixelForge Studios embraced its scrappy indie roots and focused on creating heartfelt retro adventures instead of spending the entire budget on office coffee machines and decorative exposed brick.
